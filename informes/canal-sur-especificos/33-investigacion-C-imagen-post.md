### 13.4 Destellos y patrones peligrosos: Recomendación UIT-R BT.1702-3 (11/2023)

Fuente: https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BT.1702/en (estado: **BT.1702-3 (11/2023) «In force»**; la -2 de 10/2019, «Superseded») y PDF https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bt/R-REC-BT.1702-3-202311-I!!PDF-E.pdf (leídos el 29-09-2026). Título: **«Guidance for the reduction of photosensitive epileptic seizures caused by television»**. No estaba en el repositorio. Aplica al montaje (cortes rápidos), al grafismo y a la revisión final.

- *recommends* 1: **«that broadcasting organizations should be encouraged to provide guidance to programme producers of the risks of creating television image content which may induce photosensitive epileptic seizures in susceptible viewers of television broadcasts»** (es «should be encouraged»: guía, no obligación).
- Anexo 1: **«It is therefore impossible to eliminate the risk of flashing images on television causing convulsions in viewers with photosensitive epilepsy.»**
- Guideline 1: **«When screen luminance of the darker image is below 160 cd/m2, a potentially harmful sequence of flashes occurs where there is a difference of 20 cd/m2 or more between the screen luminance of the darker and the brighter images (…) This is applicable for both standard dynamic range (SDR) and high dynamic range (HDR) programmes.»**; por encima de 160 cd/m², **«a difference of more than 1/17 Michelson contrast (…) This is applicable for HDR programmes only.»**
- **«Irrespective of luminance, a transition to or from a saturated red is also potentially harmful.»**
- **«Isolated single, double, or triple flashes are acceptable, but a sequence of flashes is not permitted when both the following occur: – the combined area of flashes occurring concurrently occupies more than 25% of the displayed (see Note 4) screen area; and – there are more than three flashes (i.e. six changes in luminance as described above) within any one-second period.»**
- **«successive flashes for which the leading edges are separated by 360 ms or more are acceptable in a 50 Hz environment or separated by 334 ms or more are acceptable in a 60 Hz environment, irrespective of their brightness or screen area.»**
- **«Rapidly changing image sequences (for example, fast cuts) are provocative if they result in areas of the screen that flash, in which case the same constraints apply as for flashes.»**
- **«…a sequence of flashing images lasting more than five seconds might constitute a risk even when it complies with the guidelines above.»**
- Guideline 2: **«Potentially harmful regular patterns occur when an image contains light and dark pairs of clearly discernible stripes in any orientation.»**
- Nota 3: SDR se supone con blanco pico de **«200 cd/m2»** y HDR HLG con **«1 000 cd/m2»**. Nota 5: **«The use of automatic video analysers to help alert production staff to potentially guideline violations in video material can be beneficial.»** Nota 1: **«There may be regional, broadcaster or content distributor variations to the guidelines given above. It is advisable that programme suppliers consult the relevant delivery requirements.»**

### 15.1 Transmedia: la definición de Jenkins

Fuente: Henry Jenkins, «Transmedia Storytelling 101», blog *Confessions of an Aca-Fan*, 21-III-2007, https://henryjenkins.org/blog/2007/03/transmedia_storytelling_101.html (leído el 29-09-2026). Es texto académico divulgativo del autor que acuñó el uso actual; no es norma.

- Punto 1: **«Transmedia storytelling represents a process where integral elements of a fiction get dispersed systematically across multiple delivery channels for the purpose of creating a unified and coordinated entertainment experience. Ideally, each medium makes it own unique contribution to the unfolding of the story.»** (así, «makes it own»). Ejemplo en el mismo punto: *The Matrix* (películas, cortos animados, cómics, videojuegos): **«There is no one single source or ur-text where one can turn to gain all of the information needed to comprehend the Matrix universe.»**
- Punto 3 (mundo narrativo, enlaza con el *storyworld* de RTVE): **«Most often, transmedia stories are based not on individual characters or specific plots but rather complex fictional worlds which can sustain multiple interrelated characters and their stories. This process of world-building encourages an encyclopedic impulse in both readers and writers.»**
- Punto 4 (funciones de las extensiones): **«Extensions may serve a variety of different functions.»** Ejemplo: **«the BBC used radio dramas to maintain audience interest in Doctor Who during almost a decade during which no new television episodes were produced.»**
- Punto 9 (participación): **«A transmedia text does not simply disperse information: it provides a set of roles and goals which readers can assume as they enact aspects of the story through their everyday life.»**
- Casa: Contrato-programa 3.3 p. 29, «registros ‘cross media’ y ‘transmedia’» (ya en 32/09).
