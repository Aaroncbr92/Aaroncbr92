FAQs 

What is Heat-Free printing and how does it work? 

Heat-Free printing is Epson’s inkjet technology that prints without using heat in the ink-delivery process. Traditional laser printers use heat to fuse toner onto the page, a process that consumes significant energy. Epson’s Heat-Free printing technology instead uses a precise mechanical process (Micro Piezo technology) that applies pressure to push ink through tiny nozzles, with no thermal stress on the printhead. This extends its lifespan, reduces energy use, and delivers consistent, high-quality output over time.

Why is Heat-Free printing better for the environment than laser printing? 

Heat-Free printing is more sustainable in several important ways. It consumes significantly less electricity than comparable laser models. Inkjet printers also generate fewer consumable waste products, with no toner cartridges, drum units, or fuser assemblies to dispose of. For organisations committed to reducing their environmental impact and meeting sustainability targets, switching to Heat-Free printing is a practical and measurable step forward.

Is Heat-Free printing cheaper to run than a laser printer? 

In most business scenarios, yes. Heat-Free printers have fewer component parts than laser devices, which means fewer items to replace, repair, or service over time. Laser printers rely on consumables such as toner cartridges, drum units, and fuser kits, all of which add ongoing cost. When you factor in considerably lower electricity usage, the total cost of ownership for a Heat-Free inkjet printer is typically much lower than an equivalent laser device.

How much energy does a Heat-Free printer use compared to a laser printer? 

Heat-Free printers use substantially less electricity than comparable laser models. This is because laser printing requires a fuser unit that generates intense heat to bond toner to paper, whereas Epson’s Heat-Free technology propels ink directly onto the page without any heating element. For businesses running high print volumes, this difference in energy consumption can translate into meaningful cost savings on electricity bills and a measurable reduction in carbon footprint.
